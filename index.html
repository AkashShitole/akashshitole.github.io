Scalable Detection of Scam and Deceptive Content in Online Video Ecosystems
University of Louisiana at Lafayette · Advisor: Prof. Bhupendra Acharya

Addressing the rapidly escalating threat of fraudulent content across online video platforms, this research designs and implements an end-to-end data collection and detection pipeline targeting scam videos on platforms such as YouTube. The project introduces a custom keyword taxonomy engineered to surface potentially deceptive content at scale, enabling systematic scraping and labeling of a large-volume dataset. Machine learning models are then trained over this corpus to analyze textual, semantic, and contextual signals — moving beyond simple keyword matching toward nuanced, multi-signal classification of adversarial content. The broader goal is to advance measurement science around online fraud ecosystems and contribute reproducible tooling for the research community.

2019
Comparison of Symmetric Cryptographic Techniques
International Journal of Research in Electronics and Computer Engineering (IJRECE) · Vol. 7, Issue 2 · ISSN: 2393-9028

Conducted a systematic comparative analysis of seven widely deployed symmetric cryptographic techniques — Transposition, Substitution, Vigenère, Block, Feistel, DES, and AES ciphers — evaluating each against key size, cipher complexity, and real-world breakability. Drawing on peer-reviewed attack literature including quantum period-finding and differential fault attacks, the study concludes that no cipher is unconditionally secure, while establishing AES (128/192/256-bit) as the strongest candidate for modern secure data transmission.
